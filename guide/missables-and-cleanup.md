# Missables, final cleanup and postgame

- Version matters: the N64 Forest Stage/Poacher's Saw warning and the old Jabu map/Ruto warning are not established remake missables. Do not treat older guides as proof. This itinerary still collects child rewards and dungeon maps during the appropriate visit for efficient routing.

- Reported bug, not an officially confirmed universal missable: players report Epona's Song may fail to be taught after acquiring the Ocarina of Time. Learn it from Malon during the early ranch visit as this route directs. Nintendo's 1.1.1 notes do not identify a fix by name.

- Timed trading: clear the intended path before taking perishables and avoid warp songs during deliveries unless the remake's current behavior has been directly confirmed. This conservative route uses ordinary travel. Keep one empty bottle when the next step requires bugs, fish or Blue Fire.

- Some completion sources disagree on changed collectible rooms and thresholds. The specific entries preserve those conflicts. They are genuine verification limits, not extra collectibles: never count both proposed rooms as two tokens.

- Historical weapons and temporary quest objects cannot all be retained at once. Buy the Giant's Knife before the final Biggoron reward if recording that purchase. Protect expensive shields and tunics from equipment-eating enemies and replace any lost equipment before the final audit.

- Export your checklist before clearing browser data or moving to a new hostname. In-game progress and this companion are independent; reloading an older game save does not roll back browser checkboxes.

## 02. Great Deku Tree: clear the whole first dungeon

- The Slingshot exit and fourth Skulltula differ from the N64 game. Collect all four tokens on this visit.

## 03. Castle Town and Zelda: the new bottle errand

- The egg, hatched Cucco and Zelda’s Letter are successive temporary quest states; collecting every state does not mean keeping all of them at once.

## 04. Lost Woods: Saria, target game and stick capacity

- Target-game source conflict: RPG Site describes three 100-point bullseyes, while ZeldaCentral describes a multiple-target clearing. Follow the remake’s displayed game and achieve its perfect score; the upgrade is verified, but this exact minigame setup is not independently settled.

## 05. Kakariko and Graveyard: Cuccos, Sun’s Song and digging

- Cuccos needed to glide to the roof are inconvenient after returning them all; take the roof reward first. Digging and racing Dampe are different activities in different ages.

## 08. Death Mountain summit: Din’s Fire and magic

- Fairy rewards have moved: Din’s Fire is at the summit in this remake, not at the old N64 Castle fountain. Pay the stated offering; older free-fountain instructions do not apply.

## 09. Ranch and Market: Epona’s Song and child games

- Bowling prize order and repeat attempts vary. Claim each unique reward once. The Skull Mask must be borrowed again if you sell it before showing the Forest Stage.

## 11. Lord Jabu-Jabu’s Belly: Ruto and Barinade

- Map and Boomerang placement changed in the remake. Collect the water-recess Map now; the original blue-switch map warning is not a verified remake missable.

## 12. Child circuit: masks, upgrades and Boomerang pickups

- Masks occupy one borrowed-mask slot; availability of every mask is the completion requirement, not simultaneous possession. The old Poacher’s Saw bug has not been verified for this remake; this route safely obtains the Forest Stage reward before adult trading anyway.

## 13. Temple of Time: enter the adult era

- Do not describe the first time jump as a permanent child-content lockout: Forest Temple unlocks returning to childhood.

## 14. Adult essentials: Epona, Hookshot and Storms

- Do not trade Cojiro yet; r23 groups the timed deliveries after the Domain has thawed. A failed Dampe race can be retried.

## 15. Forest Temple: four Poes and Phantom Ganon

- The remake adds an entrance lock, a block-room lift and a different basement torch/eye finish. Do not use the original key count as a checkpoint.

## 16. Return to childhood: Well and Hall access

- The Well is available after Forest Temple with Song of Storms. Most of its rooms are optional for story, but both key cells, all three tokens, Map, Compass and the listed side chests belong to this completion visit.

## 17. Child cleanup: storms, frogs and hidden games

- Do not waste a new time transition just for the crater bean: Bolero of Fire is obtained next, and both remote beans are grouped with child Spirit Temple in r29.

## 18. Fire Temple: Goron rescues and the Hammer

- The remake’s first Flare Dancer requires a Bomb to expose its body; do not depend on the original Hookshot-only method. This route’s keys follow its changed cells and doors.

## 20. Ice Cavern: Blue Fire and Iron Boots

- Fill a Blue Fire bottle before using the exit; otherwise thawing King Zora requires returning through the cave. Source wording disagrees on Ice Cavern token subarea names; use the attached individual collectible records for each token.

## 21. Water Temple: one planned water-level cycle

- The remake changes keys and chest contents. Follow four key pickups here; do not search for six N64 keys. Nintendo Life’s initial Ruto east/west wording is contradictory: follow the blue marked corridor. Its high central token requires Longshot, so this route deliberately postpones it until after Dark Link.

## 23. Biggoron Sword: finish all timed deliveries together

- Do not accept the frog before confirming the Domain–lake tunnel is thawed and accessible. Timed goods are successive traded states; do not try to retain them all. The Giant’s Knife is a separate breakable purchase and is not a substitute for Biggoron Sword.

- Published remake guides disagree on trade timer values. Follow the live countdown; this route targets under 3:00 for mushroom, 2:30 for frog and 3:30 for drops, so it works under the shorter reported limits.

## 24. Big Poes: all ten haunted obstacles

- Old automatic N64 spawn points and the 1,000-point bottle requirement are obsolete. In the remake: jump haunted obstacles, 30 rupees and 100 points per sale; bottle at 500, Gold Rupee at 1,000.

## 25. Gerudo Fortress: free every carpenter

- The guard’s leaping spin attack can send you back to jail; blocking alone is unreliable. A capture delays the route but does not erase freed carpenters.

## 27. Child Spirit Temple and the two remote beans

- Silver Gauntlets cannot be equipped as a child. Take the chest, then age up; do not search for a child-use control.

## 28. Adult Spirit Temple: Mirror Shield and Twinrova

- If you move the adult Time Block before taking its alcove token, move it back; this is a return-trip inconvenience rather than a permanent missable.

- Spirit comes before Shadow on this route. Use the two ordinary jumps onto the statue hand, Longshot for distant targets, and Song of Time for the Anubis-room gap; Hover Boots are collected in the next temple.

## 29. Shadow Temple: illusions, ferry and Bongo Bongo

- Carry magic for Lenses and fire. The fifth token is behind the cauldron/skull area; the ferry Scarecrow token is a separate pickup.

- This is the final main temple on this route: Twinrova and the Spirit Medallion are already complete. Finish Shadow before Proving Grounds, horseback challenges and the completion audit.

## 30. Adult regional circuit: Longshot, Hammer and beans

- This is a region circuit tied to individual collectible records, not permission to check unvisited pickups. Leave crater/Colossus bean rewards unchecked until the child planting trip.

## 31. Gerudo Proving Grounds: all keys and chests

- This is the remake’s Proving Grounds, with 30-rupee admission and new switches/chests. Seven keys can reach Ice Arrows, but the all-room route gets nine. Do not begin by spending keys down the right branch.

## 32. Horseback archery and Malon’s obstacle course

- Exact remake archery thresholds remain disputed between inspected sources. The ≥1,500 target is a conservative route goal, not a claim that the N64 thresholds are unchanged.

## 33. Dungeon and chest audit before the finale

- Exact complete chest counts have not been independently verified in every remake dungeon. The explicit chest instructions are provided, but the guide does not claim a tested exhaustive all-chest manifest.

## 35. Final cleanup: permanent rewards and optional encounters

- The guide’s core progress is its explicit objective set, not an assertion that every chest, Fairy Thought or app record has been independently catalogued. Optional records and externally gated content stay separate.

## 40. Tower, Ganondorf, escape and Ganon

- Finale lock-in starts at the tower boss door for the current attempt. The precise remake post-credits save-state behaviour remains unverified; the pre-boss manual save protects cleanup access.

## 41. Hall of Fallen Foes and optional completion records

- Hall access begins after Forest Temple and Prelude of Light, including directly as an adult; the child owl scene is only its tutorial. A full independent 84-statue checklist remains unverified, though 22 rematches and three trial rules are documented.
